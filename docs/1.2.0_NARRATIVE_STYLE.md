# Chronicle narrative style

The chronicle records what happened in short, direct prose. A typical entry is one sentence; a second sentence is used only when it adds information. It has no compulsory chronicler preamble, Ethos proverb or general lesson about authority, tradition, food or prosperity.

Three EN/TR event-body variants provide sentence-level variety. In occasional founding, war-declaration and peace entries, Ethos changes a verb or a short expression, such as `was established / came into being` or `barış / sulh`. It never supplies an unrecorded motive, insult, ceremony, dialogue or named actor. Culture remains in the reading pane's caption rather than repeated introductory formulas. The Turkish metadata heading is **Karakter**; English uses **Ethos**.

An accepted new-building or upgrade commission is an order, not a completed building. Completion is a separate event. A shipment account means the cargo was received. Shared war and conquest records retain the actual declarer, victor, former owner and target while changing the viewpoint. Strategic battles remain explicitly strategic, without invented NPC deaths, loot or physical fighting.

Historical names are inserted literally. Known building names appear directly in quotation marks. Turkish avoids guessing suffixes for generated names. Dates, raw facts, current political ownership and historical locations remain separate metadata; no narrative phrase changes the authoritative record.

Event identity and participant identity still choose one of three stable variants. The renderer is deterministic and read-only: it invokes no gameplay services, external AI, network or world scan. Existing saved events use the new presentation without any history-file rewrite.
