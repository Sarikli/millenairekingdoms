## Implemented ocean atlas and limits

Production observations use only the server-observed outdoor player's nearby, already loaded columns: actual surface block/fluid, height, ocean/river/beach biome and sea level. Beach water becomes ocean only through a bounded continuous loaded-water connection to a real ocean seed. Unknown, land, ocean and other water remain distinct; missing geography is never invented. No production chunk creation, generation, tickets, global scan or client-supplied observation coordinates.

Per-player/dimension two-bit masks use 32×32 one-block tiles (256 bytes), radius 48, at most 64 primary columns/player and 256/20 ticks globally; coastal supporting reads are also bounded. Caps include 16 sessions, 128 hot masks/session, 16,384 tiles/player/dimension, 64-tile response pages and one 1024×1024 viewport raster job. Protocol 8 accepts only bounded own-cache revision windows. Atomic format 1 acknowledges immutable tiles/shards/root before publication, preserves invalid/future/foreign/contended files and uses bounded background cold loading.

Live ticks now send changed masks to **one own-file writer with at most four pending jobs**, while main-thread world acquisition remains bounded. Pending sessions cannot be read/evicted until completion; queries receive a retry. The worker retains no Minecraft world/player objects. A controlled gate proves sampling returns while disk work is blocked and no unacknowledged mask is published. Shutdown has a five-second total acknowledgement deadline and preserves prior cache evidence on failure.

The client draws observed ground, translucent politics, then opaque blue ocean/coast masking, with villages/capitals/names/war/history markers above. Unknown terrain stays unknown. Ocean masks hide political fills/borders over known sea without changing political ownership. One connection/dimension cache, bounded asynchronous viewport textures, pan/zoom reprojection, a simple layer toggle and late-response cleanup are integrated. [Detailed design, bounds, failure findings and performance](1.2.0_OCEAN_ATLAS.md).

The real vanilla-noise shore at **345,79** retains mask SHA-256 `ca9929a00afc1d3101963f1cbdf079ccf1e6ee96b5f0abec30e63d64317e1bfc` on separate-JVM reopen. Dev12's controlled direct observation/query loaded counts are **1114→1114**, forced tickets **0→0**. The four-tile response is **1,420 actual payload-codec bytes**, versus a separate **3,711-byte accounted-NBT estimate**. The diagnostic coast PNG is a CPU raster of real observations, not a Minecraft screenshot.

The small gated-worker fixture measures main sampling at 0.132/0.297 ms in its two JVMs. Direct synchronous validation deliberately includes durable writes: 160 calls total 776.3 ms, maximum 50.70 ms, whole fixture 888.8 ms. Earlier cold calls reached 225 ms before live persistence moved off-thread. These scopes differ; no speedup factor or hard latency/TPS/FPS guarantee is claimed. The 10 ms budget is soft and checked between players. Synthetic large-cache sharding measurements remain separately labeled in the design document.
