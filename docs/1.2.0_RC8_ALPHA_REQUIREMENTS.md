1. MAIN OBJECTIVE

Expand the existing 1.2.0 release candidate with all previously planned 1.3.0 historical chronicle and political map features.

These features must now become part of 1.2.0 — By Royal Decree, not a separate 1.3.0 release.

The final experience should combine:

1. Autonomous kingdom economy and development.
2. Kingdom Ethos / Töre.
3. A standalone political world atlas.
4. Persistent kingdom and world history.
5. Literary medieval chronicles describing genuine simulation events.
6. Integrated navigation between the existing kingdom GUI, History screen and political atlas.

The guiding philosophy remains unchanged:

Millénaire provides autonomous village life. Millénaire Kingdoms provides the political world, institutions and history emerging from those villages.

Do not replace original Millénaire autonomy with scripted behavior.

This is an implementation assignment, not merely a request for a design proposal. Inspect the actual repository, implement incrementally, test, document and commit the work.

5. PERSISTENT POLITICAL HISTORY ENGINE

Implement an authoritative, versioned historical event system.

Record historical events only after their corresponding gameplay transitions are successfully committed.

Required event types:

- Kingdom founding.
- Peaceful diplomatic village incorporation.
- War declaration.
- Peace agreement.
- Village conquest.
- Capital conquest.
- Kingdom collapse.
- Ruler claiming or relinquishing authority.
- Royal construction order.
- Actual royal construction completion.
- Significant economic investment.
- Significant goods shipment.

Avoid excessive history entries for routine taxes or repetitive everyday operations.

Each event should contain:

- Unique persistent identifier.
- Event type.
- Minecraft day and relevant game time.
- Dimension where applicable.
- Involved kingdom IDs.
- Involved village IDs.
- Historical display names.
- Relevant ruler/actor if genuinely known.
- Source event or operation identity.
- Verified reasons or conditions where available.
- Outcome and relevant factual context.
- Historical location where meaningful.

Persistence requirements

- No duplicated history entries after retries or reloads.
- No successful history entry for a failed action.
- No fabricated historical events when upgrading older worlds.
- Preserve destroyed kingdoms' historical records.
- Preserve names and identities even after political entities disappear.
- Protect unknown and future data.
- Maintain separate, bounded historical storage.
- Avoid expanding the existing political SavedData into an unmanageable monolith.

Ensure important history events can survive server restarts without modifying financial or original Millénaire save mechanisms.

6. MEDIEVAL NARRATIVE CHRONICLE ENGINE

This is a central feature of the expansion.

Chronicles must not read like plain debug logs.

They should resemble historical accounts written by medieval chroniclers describing political events within their own world.

Use vivid but controlled literary language appropriate to an approximately 11th-century setting.

The narration may contain:

- Political interpretation.
- Rhetorical emphasis.
- Dynastic or royal prestige.
- Religious language where culturally appropriate.
- Reports from distant regions.
- Rumors and hearsay.
- Different interpretations of the same event.
- Celebratory or mournful literary tone.

However:

The chronicler may be biased; the underlying historical records must remain factual.

Example: Peaceful incorporation

Instead of:

"Day 81: Bellefontaine joined Ondefontaine."

Prefer something resembling:

"Relations of friendship between Ondefontaine and Bellefontaine had grown steadily over time. At last, the leaders of Bellefontaine accepted the kingdom's protection, and the destinies of both settlements became bound beneath one crown."

Example: War declaration

Instead of:

"Day 137: Ondefontaine declared war."

Prefer:

"Word reached the court of Ondefontaine that the neighboring ruler had shown grave disrespect toward the kingdom's traditions. Whether every accusation was true remained uncertain, yet tensions between the two realms had become impossible to ignore. The rulers of Ondefontaine declared that the time for peaceful restraint had ended, and war was proclaimed."

Important: Religious insults, border atrocities or diplomatic incidents must never be invented as verified historical facts.

If such events are not established by actual simulation data, the chronicle must not falsely attribute them to an opponent.

Use only grounded reasons or safe literary formulations that do not assert imaginary occurrences.

Example: Royal construction investment

"The royal administration, seeking to strengthen Bellefontaine's prosperity, committed part of its treasury toward the establishment of a new smithy. The commission was entrusted to the village, whose craftsmen would determine how and when the work could be completed."

Construction orders and actual construction completions must be recorded and narrated separately.

7. PERSPECTIVE-BASED HISTORIOGRAPHY

A single event should support different narratives depending on the kingdom whose chronicle is being read.

For example, when Kingdom A attacks Kingdom B:

Kingdom A's chronicle:

"The rulers of A considered the growing strength of their neighbor a threat to the peace of the realm and resolved to take up arms."

Kingdom B's chronicle:

"The peace between the kingdoms was broken when the rulers of A declared war upon B."

Both accounts describe the same factual war declaration, but from different political perspectives.

Ethos and culture

Use a kingdom's Ethos / Töre to influence narrative emphasis.

Warlike chronicles may emphasize honor, courage, arms and military obligations.

Traditionalist chronicles may emphasize ancestry, sacred traditions and continuity.

Peaceful chronicles may emphasize stability, harvests and the burdens of war.

Industrious chronicles may emphasize prosperity, craftsmanship and commerce.

Pragmatic chronicles may emphasize strategic necessity, negotiation and political balance.

Culture should influence appropriate terminology where safely supported.

Do not present all cultures as identical European feudal societies.

Avoid modern political rhetoric and repetitive generic fantasy prose.

Implementation

Use a rich template-based deterministic narrative engine.

No external generative AI API or Internet connectivity should be required.

- Multiple narrative variants per event.
- Stable selection across reloads.
- Distinct English and Turkish prose.
- Safe contextual placeholders.
- Proper treatment of Turkish grammar and suffixes.
- No hallucinated names, incidents or activities.
- Separation of historical facts from presentation text.

Design the engine for future additions such as diplomacy, vassalage, succession, social estates and political reforms.

9. INDEPENDENT POLITICAL ATLAS

Implement a standalone, full-screen political map inspired by classic medieval strategy-game political maps.

No dependency on Xaero, JourneyMap or any external mapping mod.

Required features

- Persistent kingdom colors.
- Political influence regions.
- Visible territorial borders.
- Separate neutral/independent villages.
- Distinct capital markers.
- Village markers.
- Village-type icons.
- Kingdom names.
- Kingdom list and legend.
- Zoom and pan.
- Hover and selection.
- Selected kingdom highlighting.
- Current war indicators.
- Kingdom information panel.
- Navigation to related chronicle entries.

Territorial geometry

Generate territories from actual settlement coordinates, kingdom membership and capital information.

Consider a configurable village influence radius, initially around 500 blocks, as a design starting point rather than a rigid gameplay rule.

Neighboring political influence areas may visually form continuous regions.

Distant isolated settlements must remain separate rather than being joined by unrealistic corridors.

A clipped Voronoi-like or comparable territory algorithm may be used if appropriate.

These are visual political territories, not real land claims or changes to native Millénaire village management.

Do not modify:

- Village building boundaries.
- NPC goals.
- Village economies.
- Original settlement ownership.
- Original AI behavior.

Map performance

- Do not scan every chunk.
- Do not force-load villages.
- Use registered settlement information.
- Cache expensive geometry.
- Update the atlas when relevant political state changes.
- Support large worlds and many settlements.
- Respect dimension boundaries.
- Define server-controlled visibility rules.
- Do not accidentally expose confidential or undiscovered coordinates.

11. HISTORY–MAP INTERACTION

The chronicle and atlas should work together.

Examples:

- Clicking a kingdom in the history screen highlights it on the map.
- Clicking a village conquest event locates the village.
- Clicking a kingdom's foundation event locates its historical capital.
- Clicking a settlement reveals relevant political history.
- Clicking a collapsed kingdom opens its surviving historical archive.

Clearly distinguish past events from present ownership.

Do not depict current borders as though they were historical borders from an earlier date.

Animated political history playback is not required for this version.
