**Create an autonomous medieval political simulation where NPC kingdoms continue making strategic decisions, investing in their economies, conducting wars and changing territorial ownership even when no player is near their settlements.**

Minecraft's physical chunk-loading system must not unnecessarily prevent the political simulation from progressing.

However, the mod must never fabricate physical NPC activity, construction, money transactions or inventory changes when the relevant Minecraft objects are unavailable.

This distinction is fundamental.

# 7. SEPARATE STRATEGIC DECISION-MAKING FROM PHYSICAL EXECUTION

The central architecture must distinguish four stages.

## Stage A — Strategic planning

A kingdom evaluates its political and economic needs.

This must be possible without a player standing near the capital, using verified persisted data where sufficient.

Examples:

- Prioritize agricultural development.
- Plan defensive infrastructure.
- Identify productive infrastructure needs.
- Plan improvement of a member village.
- Consider providing logistical support for a known construction shortage.

This is a strategic decision, not a completed transaction.

## Stage B — Authorization

The proposed action is checked against current kingdom legitimacy and the available authoritative information.

Some checks may need to wait until the target village or treasury becomes loaded.

Do not mistake historical snapshots for current spendable funds.

## Stage C — Physical execution

When required Minecraft objects become safely available:

- Revalidate the native construction plan.
- Revalidate builders.
- Revalidate original materials and inventories.
- Revalidate treasury funds.
- Revalidate government authority.
- Revalidate all financial restrictions.
- Submit the action through the existing domain service.

Do not create free buildings or materials.

Do not bypass the original Millénaire construction engine.

## Stage D — Completion

A building is complete only when native construction actually finishes and the required persistent evidence exists.

Planning, authorization, payment and physical completion must remain distinct lifecycle events.

Implement two interoperable military resolution modes.

## Mode A — Native physical raid

When the required villages, entities and runtime conditions are genuinely active:

Use the original Millénaire raid/combat system.

Preserve:

- Native raiders.
- Existing physical combat.
- Legitimate target selection.
- Native raid outcomes.
- Relevant existing conquest logic.
- Normal NPC behavior.

Do not replace functional physical raids with arbitrary strategic results.

## Mode B — Strategic unattended battle

When physical raids cannot operate because the necessary regions are inactive or unloaded:

Use a server-authoritative strategic battle resolution system.

This system must rely on legitimate military capabilities and opposed dice rolls.

An NPC war should be able to progress without a player visiting either participating village.

The mode transition must be safe and persistent.

A single military operation must never receive both a physical and strategic resolution.

# 16. OPPOSED-DICE COMBAT SYSTEM

The requested system is explicitly based on **two competing dice rolls**, adjusted by military strength.

A stronger army should generally be more likely to win, but weaker armies should retain a chance to achieve an unexpected victory.

The outcome must not be determined entirely by raw military strength.

## Battle sequence

1. Verify an active legitimate war.
2. Verify a valid attacker and defender.
3. Verify an eligible military operation.
4. Obtain attacking strength.
5. Obtain defending strength.
6. Calculate relative military advantage.
7. Convert that advantage into a bounded dice modifier.
8. Roll independently for both participants.
9. Add applicable modifiers.
10. Compare results.
11. Determine battle winner.
12. Persist the result exactly once.
13. Apply legitimate military/political consequences.
14. Record the actual event in Chronicles.

## Proposed d20 model

Use one d20 roll for the attacker and one d20 roll for the defender.

An initial suggested relative-strength modifier is:

`modifier = clamp(round(6 × log2(AttackStrength / DefenseStrength)), -8, +8)`

If the modifier is positive, apply its magnitude to the attacker.

If negative, apply its magnitude to the defender.

Give the defender an additional baseline defensive advantage, initially +2.

For example:

Attack strength: 80  
Defense strength: 60

Relative advantage: approximately +2 for the attacker.

Attacker result:

`d20 + 2`

Defender result:

`d20 + 2`

Higher result wins.

An exact tie favors the defender.

This formula is a starting proposal.

Evaluate its actual statistical behavior against the real ranges of native Millénaire village military strength.

Adjust and document the final formula where appropriate.

Do not blindly preserve these numbers if they produce unbalanced outcomes.

## Required properties

- Stronger forces win more frequently.
- Weaker forces retain meaningful chances where eligible.
- Defensive specialization matters.
- Military investment matters.
- The dice remain important.
- Strength differences are not ignored.
- Modifiers do not grow without limits.
- Military-ineligible settlements cannot attack merely by rolling a natural 20.
- Invalid or zero strength is handled safely.
- No division-by-zero or numerical overflow.
- The result is determined by a server-authoritative random draw.
- A committed result cannot be rerolled after restart.

Use reasonable configurable balancing values.

Do not introduce excessively complicated combat phases for 1.2.0.
